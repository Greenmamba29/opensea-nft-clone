# Historic Reference — researched-backed truth for GrahmOS governance

## Why this skill exists

GrahmOS governance is built on the documented legacy of African American
economic achievement. Figures like Reginald Lewis and Malcolm X function as
the project's **principles board**: their *verified* work defines the
governance values (ownership, self-determination, community capital). That
foundation only holds if every historical claim is true and citable. One
apocryphal quote or inflated attribution in an investor deck invites a
fact-check that discredits the entire governance story — and disrespects the
real achievements, which need no embellishment. The documented record is
extraordinary on its own.

So the rule is simple: **the truth is the asset.** This skill exists to
protect it.

## The verification protocol

Before any historical claim enters a GrahmOS document:

1. **Start from the dossier.** `references/figures.md` holds pre-verified
   facts with sources for the core figures. If the claim is there, cite it
   and move on.
2. **New claims need two independent reputable sources.** Acceptable:
   primary documents (patents, court records, SEC filings, autobiographies),
   museum and archive institutions (Smithsonian NMAAHC, National Park
   Service, the Reginald F. Lewis Museum, the Lewis Latimer House Museum),
   major-press obituaries, and scholarly biographies. Not acceptable as the
   only source: listicles, social media graphics, AI summaries, or any page
   that itself cites none.
3. **Quote discipline.** A quotation must trace to a named primary source
   (a speech with date and place, a book with page, an interview with
   outlet). If the best you can find is "attributed to," either drop the
   quote or print it as *attributed, unverified* — never as fact.
4. **Reject apocrypha explicitly.** When a popular claim fails
   verification, do not silently omit it — record it in the dossier's
   "Common apocrypha" section with a one-line correction, so it cannot creep
   back in through a future edit. (Example already recorded: Lewis Latimer
   did not invent the lightbulb, and there is no documented
   Latimer–Einstein collaboration or jointly discovered algorithm. His real,
   documented work — the 1882 carbon-filament process patent, the Bell
   telephone patent drawings — is remarkable and fully citable.)
5. **Calibrate the language to the evidence.** "First Black-owned company
   to surpass $1B in revenue" (documented) reads differently from "widely
   regarded as" (consensus) and from "attributed" (unverified). Use the
   weakest phrasing the evidence forces, and the strongest it allows.

## How figures map to governance

When drafting governance content, connect each principle to a *documented*
practice of a figure, not to vibes:

| Governance principle | Grounded in (documented) |
|---|---|
| Tenants own appreciating equity, not just rent | Reginald Lewis's leveraged acquisitions (McCall, TLC Beatrice) — ownership as the wealth engine |
| Economic self-determination of the community | Malcolm X, "The Ballot or the Bullet" (Apr 3, 1964): community ownership and control of its own economy |
| Build the sales network, not just the product | Madam C.J. Walker's commissioned sales-agent system |
| Banking and credit inside the community | Maggie Lena Walker, St. Luke Penny Savings Bank (1903) |
| Infrastructure and patient improvement | Lewis Latimer's carbon-filament manufacturing process (U.S. Patent 252,386) |
| Commercial districts as engines (the mall thesis itself) | Greenwood / "Black Wall Street," Tulsa |

When a document needs a figure or fact not in the dossier, run the
verification protocol, then **add the verified entry to
`references/figures.md`** so the next writer inherits it.

## Output rules for governance documents

- Every historical claim carries an inline source: `(source: …)` or a
  footnote — author, work, year; or institution + page.
- Never rank or compare figures' "greatness"; present documented
  achievements.
- Dates, dollar amounts, and "first" claims are the highest-risk items —
  verify each one individually, every time.
- If a stakeholder (including the founder) supplies a historical claim that
  fails verification, say so plainly, offer the documented alternative, and
  use the documented version in the artifact.
