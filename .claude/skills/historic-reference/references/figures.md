# Vetted dossiers — documented facts with sources

Every entry below passed the two-source rule. When citing in a GrahmOS
document, carry the source forward. When adding an entry, follow the
verification protocol in SKILL.md and append here.

## Reginald F. Lewis (1942–1993)

- Harvard Law School, J.D. 1968; began on Wall Street at Paul, Weiss before
  founding his own firm — one of the first African American–led law firms on
  Wall Street.
- 1984: through TLC Group, acquired McCall Pattern Company for ~$22.5M
  (mostly leveraged); sold it in 1987 for ~$65M plus retained real estate —
  roughly a 90-to-1 return on invested equity.
- 1987: led the ~$985M leveraged buyout of Beatrice International Foods —
  creating **TLC Beatrice International**, the first Black-owned company to
  exceed $1 billion in annual revenue (it reached ~$2B by the early 1990s).
- 1992: gave $3M to Harvard Law School — at the time the largest single
  grant in HLS history; the Reginald F. Lewis International Law Center was
  the first Harvard building named for an African American.
- Sources: Lewis & Walker, *"Why Should White Guys Have All the Fun?"*
  (1995); Reginald F. Lewis Museum of Maryland African American History &
  Culture (Baltimore); New York Times obituary, Jan 20 1993.

## Malcolm X (1925–1965) — economic principles

- "The Ballot or the Bullet" (Cleveland, April 3, 1964; also Detroit,
  April 12, 1964): the economic philosophy of Black nationalism — the
  community should own, operate, and control the businesses and economy of
  its own community, so that wealth and jobs recirculate within it.
- Founded the Organization of Afro-American Unity (OAAU), 1964, whose
  program included economic self-reliance, consumer education, and community
  control of commerce.
- *The Autobiography of Malcolm X* (1965, with Alex Haley) documents the
  evolution of this economic program.
- Sources: speech transcripts in *Malcolm X Speaks* (Breitman ed., 1965);
  *The Autobiography of Malcolm X* (1965); OAAU founding rally transcript,
  June 28, 1964.
- Citation note: quote him only from dated, transcribed speeches or the
  Autobiography — Malcolm X is among the most misquoted figures on the
  internet.

## Madam C.J. Walker (Sarah Breedlove, 1867–1919)

- Built the Madam C.J. Walker Manufacturing Company (hair-care products,
  the "Walker System"); headquarters and factory in Indianapolis from 1910.
- Built a national direct-sales force of thousands of commissioned
  African American women sales agents — an income ladder, not just a product.
- Widely documented (incl. Guinness World Records) as the first female
  self-made millionaire in America; funded the NAACP anti-lynching campaign
  and Black institutions.
- Sources: A'Lelia Bundles, *On Her Own Ground* (2001 — written by her
  great-great-granddaughter from family archives); Madam Walker Legacy
  Center (Indianapolis); NMAAHC collections.

## Lewis H. Latimer (1848–1928)

- Son of parents who escaped slavery; self-taught master draftsman.
- 1876: drafted the patent drawings for Alexander Graham Bell's telephone
  patent application.
- 1882: U.S. Patent 252,386, "Process of Manufacturing Carbons" — an
  improved method of producing durable carbon filaments, which helped make
  incandescent lighting commercially practical.
- Worked for Hiram Maxim's U.S. Electric Lighting Co., then for the Edison
  Electric Light Co., including as a patent expert witness in infringement
  litigation; member of the Edison Pioneers (1918).
- Sources: Lewis Latimer House Museum (Flushing, NY); USPTO patent record
  252,386; Edison Pioneers records; Fouché, *Black Inventors in the Age of
  Segregation* (2003).

## Maggie Lena Walker (1864–1934)

- 1903: chartered and led the St. Luke Penny Savings Bank in Richmond, VA —
  the first African American woman to charter a U.S. bank and serve as its
  president; the successor institution survived the Depression.
- Built the Independent Order of St. Luke into a financial and publishing
  enterprise employing Black women.
- Sources: National Park Service, Maggie L. Walker National Historic Site;
  Marlowe, *A Right Worthy Grand Mission* (2003).

## A.G. Gaston (1892–1996)

- Birmingham entrepreneur: Booker T. Washington Insurance Company, Citizens
  Federal Savings Bank, business college, funeral homes, and the A.G. Gaston
  Motel — which served as headquarters for the 1963 Birmingham civil rights
  campaign; Gaston repeatedly posted bail for movement leaders.
- Memoir: *Green Power: The Successful Way of A.G. Gaston* (1968).
- Sources: *Green Power* (1968); Jenkins & Hines, *Black Titan: A.G. Gaston
  and the Making of a Black American Millionaire* (2004); NPS Birmingham
  Civil Rights National Monument records.

## John H. Johnson (1918–2005)

- Founded Johnson Publishing Company: *Negro Digest* (1942), *Ebony* (1945),
  *Jet* (1951) — built the dominant Black media enterprise of the century.
- 1982: first African American on the Forbes 400.
- Sources: Johnson, *Succeeding Against the Odds* (1989); Forbes 400 (1982);
  New York Times obituary, Aug 8 2005.

## Annie Turnbo Malone (1869–1957)

- Founded Poro Company (hair care) and Poro College (1918, St. Louis) — a
  training institution that created tens of thousands of jobs for Black
  women; an early mentor/employer of Sarah Breedlove (Madam C.J. Walker).
- Major philanthropist (Howard University Medical School, St. Louis Colored
  Orphans' Home — later renamed for her).
- Sources: Missouri Historical Society; NMAAHC collections.

## Greenwood District, Tulsa — "Black Wall Street"

- Early-1900s Black commercial district founded on land developments by
  O.W. Gurley and built up by entrepreneurs incl. J.B. Stradford (Stradford
  Hotel): dozens of Black-owned groceries, hotels, theaters, professional
  offices — a self-contained commercial ecosystem where dollars recirculated.
- Destroyed in the Tulsa Race Massacre (May 31–June 1, 1921); partially
  rebuilt by its own community in the following years.
- Sources: Oklahoma Commission to Study the Tulsa Race Riot of 1921 (2001
  report); Greenwood Cultural Center; Tulsa Historical Society.
- Citation note: this is the closest documented ancestor of the GrahmOS
  mall thesis — a commercial district as a community wealth engine. Cite the
  ecosystem and the documented destruction; avoid invented statistics about
  "dollars circulating N times," which are not supported by period records.

## Garrett Morgan (1877–1963)

- 1914: patented a safety hood / smoke protection device; used it himself in
  the 1916 Lake Erie waterworks tunnel rescue in Cleveland.
- 1923: U.S. Patent 1,475,024 — a three-position traffic signal; sold to
  General Electric.
- Sources: USPTO patents 1,113,675 and 1,475,024; Western Reserve Historical
  Society; NMAAHC.

# Common apocrypha — do not use

Claims that circulate widely and fail verification. If one appears in a
draft, replace it with the documented alternative noted here.

| Apocryphal claim | The documented truth to use instead |
|---|---|
| "Lewis Latimer invented the lightbulb" | He patented an improved carbon-filament *manufacturing process* (1882) that helped make incandescent lighting practical and durable. |
| "Latimer and Einstein discovered an algorithm / quant theory together" | No documented connection exists — different fields, and Latimer's career predates Einstein's U.S. years (Latimer died 1928; Einstein emigrated 1933). Latimer's real record needs no embellishment. |
| Unsourced viral Malcolm X quotes | Quote only from dated speech transcripts (*Malcolm X Speaks*) or the Autobiography. |
| "Madam Walker was the first American woman millionaire" | Documented claim is first *female self-made* millionaire in America (Guinness; Bundles); other women were millionaires earlier via inheritance. |
| Precise "dollar circulated 36×/19×" figures for Black Wall Street | Not supported by period records; describe the documented self-contained commercial ecosystem instead. |
