# Built on a Legacy of Black Commerce

### GrahmOS stands in a tradition of community-built marketplaces — we're rebuilding the digital Main Street they pioneered.

---

## Annie Turnbo Malone (1869–1957)
**Founder of Poro, one of America's first Black-owned beauty empires**

> **~75,000 agents** — At its peak in the 1920s, Malone's Poro network trained and deployed an estimated 75,000 sales agents across the U.S., Caribbean, and beyond — a distributed commerce platform decades before the internet.

*Why it matters for GrahmOS:* Malone proved that an agent-led, guided-selling model scales. Our AI guides play the role her trained agents did — personal, trusted, and everywhere the customer is.

---

## Black Wall Street — Greenwood District, Tulsa (c. 1906–1921)
**The most concentrated Black business district in American history**

> **190+ businesses** — Greenwood's roughly 35-block district supported more than 190 Black-owned businesses, including hotels, theaters, groceries, law offices, and two newspapers, serving a community of ~10,000 residents.

*Why it matters for GrahmOS:* Greenwood showed what happens when a community owns its own marketplace end to end. GrahmOS is digital Greenwood — merchant-owned storefronts, community-circulated dollars, AI-guided foot traffic.

### Sourcing note (remove before presenting)
- The ~75,000 Poro agents figure is the commonly cited number in historical accounts (e.g., NMAAHC, Missouri Historical Society); contemporary records are imprecise, so cite it as "an estimated 75,000."
- The 190+ businesses figure aligns with the 1920 Tulsa city directory and Tulsa Historical Society / Greenwood Rising accounts; some sources round to "nearly 200."
- Two popular deck claims were deliberately excluded as unverifiable: that Malone was "America's first Black woman millionaire worth $14M" (her exact net worth is disputed in the historical record) and that "a dollar circulated 36 times / stayed in Greenwood for a year" (no primary source exists).
